\subsection{The value metric, not the latent distance}
\label{sec:tworoom}

\paragraph{Setup.}
TwoRoom is a $2$-D navigation task from $224\times224$ RGB: reach a target in
the opposite room through one of up to three doorways, success within $16$ px.
All planners optimize $N = 5$ action blocks ($25$ primitive steps) and execute
the whole sequence before replanning. We use three pretrained world models
that differ in how much geometry their latent carries---two encoding an
observation into a single $192$-d \texttt{CLS} token (LeWM, PLDM) and DINO-WM
\citep{zhou2024dinowm}, which keeps $196$ patch tokens ($77{,}224$-d)---and
plan under two costs. The \emph{latent distance}
$\lVert \hat z_N - \hat z_g\rVert_2^2$ is the objective the released
checkpoints ship with. The \emph{value metric} is our critic
$V(\hat z,\hat z_g) = d_\psi(\hat z \!\to\! \hat z_g)$, a metric residual
network \citep{liu2022metric} whose symmetric and asymmetric parts are the two
halves of one $128$-d embedding, so it is a quasimetric and composes long
distances out of short transitions; it is trained on cached latents by $n$-step
TD with hindsight relabeling \citep{andrychowicz2017her} ($n = 50$,
undiscounted, expectile $0.1$). Evaluation replays offline trajectories
($10{,}000$ episodes): start $=$ row $t$, goal $=$ row $t+\Delta$, so $\Delta$
sets the goal distance. We report $\Delta = 25$ ($h25$, one horizon) and
$\Delta = 50$ ($h50$, two), $50$ tasks per cell, $3$ task seeds $\times$ $3$
initializations for every learned component. All three models reproduce their
published $h25$ rates (ours vs.\ reported: PLDM $96.7/97$, LeWM $89.3/87$,
DINO-WM $100.0/100$).

\paragraph{The latent distance is not a planning objective.}
Read at $h25$ alone---the horizon these benchmarks publish---the two costs in
Table~\ref{tab:tworoom} look interchangeable and every model looks solved
($89$--$100$). Doubling the goal distance separates them: under the latent
distance CEM loses $34.6$ points on LeWM and $19.4$ on PLDM, while under the
value metric nothing degrades. This is a property of the objective, not of
tuning. Squared latent distance measures how similar two encodings look, which
tracks reachability only locally; as the goal recedes its minimizer stops being
the plan that arrives. A quasimetric fit to temporal distance orders states by
steps-to-go by construction. How much that is worth scales inversely with the
latent: the $192$-d single-token models lose $34.6$ and $19.4$ points and gain
$45.1$ and $22.3$ from the critic, whereas DINO-WM's $196$ tokens lose $2.0$ and
gain $2.0$. The converse also holds---on a latent rich enough to make the native
distance informative even the untuned sampler succeeds ($95.3/96.7$ on DINO-WM
against $65.3/47.3$ on LeWM)---so the critic is not what makes planning work in
general, but what makes it work on representations that do not already encode
the geometry.

\paragraph{Refinement, not the critic alone, buys the search budget back.}
Holding the cost fixed at the value metric and varying only the query budget,
RLP matches CEM to within noise at ${\sim}190\times$ fewer queries, and degrades
gracefully: $K$ halves to $4$ for free on both single-token models and to $2$ at
a cost of $3.8$ points on PLDM and none on LeWM. CEM's own knob does nothing
between $10$ and $30$ iterations---$6{,}000$ wasted rollouts. The last row
isolates the recursion itself: a policy differing from RLP only in that it emits
its plan in one pass collapses to $31$--$38$ at $h25$ and $15$--$23$ at $h50$,
below every arm including the untuned sampler, while two refinement steps over
the identical value landscape recover $93$--$100$ at comparable cost. Since
replanning five times as often moves it by at most $5.6$ points, it is the
refinement recursion~\eqref{eq:plan-rollout}--\eqref{eq:plan-refine}, not the
learned value alone, that makes amortized planning work.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Critic and planner are trained on latents cached from the same episodes the
evaluation draws tasks from, so all learned rows are in-distribution fits rather
than generalization estimates; memorization is implausible (${\sim}150$ tasks
against ${\sim}2$M valid start--goal pairs) and the effects are an order of
magnitude above the optimism attributable to selection at this sample size, but
the held-out card is not run. The latent-distance rows contain no learned
component and are unaffected. Several arms sit at $99$--$100$, where a
$50$-episode cell cannot discriminate ($\mathrm{SE} \approx 2.8$), so two-point
differences inside that band are not an ordering. TwoRoom is deliberately easy
for the optimizer, which is what isolates the critic here; the planner's own
contribution is measured in Section~\ref{sec:highdim}.
