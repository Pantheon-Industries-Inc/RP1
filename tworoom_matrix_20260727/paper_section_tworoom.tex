\section{Results}

We evaluate in two stages. Section~\ref{sec:tworoom} isolates the
\emph{critic}: on a navigation task where every planner is strong enough that
the optimizer is not the bottleneck, we show that the objective conventionally
used with latent world models---distance in latent space---is inherently
flawed, that a learned value metric repairs it, and that RLP matches the best
sampling planner while querying the world model two to three orders of
magnitude less. Section~\ref{sec:highdim} then scales to Reacher and OGBench,
where the action space is large enough that the \emph{planner} becomes the
bottleneck.

\subsection{The value metric, not the latent distance}
\label{sec:tworoom}

\paragraph{Setup.}
TwoRoom is a $2$-D navigation task: a circular agent must reach a target in the
opposite room through one of up to three doorways in a dividing wall, from
$224 \times 224$ RGB observations, with success declared within $16$ px and at
most $100$ primitive steps. Actions are $2$-D velocities, grouped into blocks
of $5$ by the frameskip; all planners use $N = 5$ action blocks
($25$ primitive steps) and execute the entire optimized sequence before
replanning. We use three pretrained latent world models that differ in how much
geometry their latent carries: two that encode an observation into a single
$192$-d \texttt{CLS} token (LeWM, PLDM) and DINO-WM
\citep{zhou2024dinowm}, which keeps $196$ patch tokens ($77{,}224$-d flat).
The offline dataset is $10{,}000$ demonstration episodes ($920{,}809$ frames).
Evaluation replays dataset rows: the start is row $t$ and the goal is row
$t + \Delta$, so $\Delta$ directly controls how far the goal is. We report
$\Delta = 25$ ($h25$, one planning horizon away) and $\Delta = 50$ ($h50$, two),
$50$ tasks per cell, averaged over $3$ task seeds and---for every learned
component---$3$ independent initializations.

\paragraph{Two costs.}
The \emph{latent distance} baseline scores a plan by
$\lVert \hat z_N - \hat z_g \rVert_2^2$, the objective the released checkpoints
are planned with. The \emph{value metric} is our critic
$V(\hat z, \hat z_g) = d_\psi(\hat z \to \hat z_g)$, realized as a metric
residual network \citep{liu2022metric}: a quasimetric whose symmetric and
asymmetric parts are the two halves of one $128$-d embedding, so it is
non-negative and satisfies the triangle inequality and therefore composes long
distances out of short transitions. It is trained on cached latents by $n$-step
temporal-difference learning with hindsight goal relabeling
\citep{andrychowicz2017her} ($n = 50$, undiscounted, expectile $0.1$), and is
frozen when it scores plans for the sampling planners. RLP is the residual
planner of Section~\ref{sec:dyna} with $K = 8$ refinement steps, whose critic is
warm-started from that same checkpoint and co-trained with the planner.

All three world models reproduce their published TwoRoom success rates under
this protocol at $h25$ (ours vs.\ reported): PLDM $96.7$ vs.\ $97$, LeWM $89.3$
vs.\ $87$, DINO-WM $100.0$ vs.\ $100$, which fixes the reference frame for
everything that follows.

\paragraph{The latent distance is not a planning objective.}
Table~\ref{tab:tworoom-matrix} crosses the two costs with three optimizers.
Read at $h25$ alone---the horizon at which these benchmarks are published---the
two costs look interchangeable and every base looks solved: $89$--$100$
everywhere. Doubling the goal distance separates them completely. Under the
latent distance, CEM falls by $34.6$ points on LeWM and $19.4$ on PLDM, while
under the value metric no arm degrades at all.

The failure is not a tuning artifact but a property of the objective. Squared
latent distance measures how similar two encodings look, which coincides with
reachability only in a neighbourhood; as the goal recedes, the objective's
minimizer stops being the plan that gets there. A quasimetric trained on
temporal distance orders states by steps-to-go by construction, and it is that
ordering---not a better optimizer---that survives the longer horizon.

\paragraph{How much the critic is worth depends on the latent.}
Table~\ref{tab:tworoom-degradation} orders the three world models by latent
capacity. The degradation of the latent distance, and correspondingly the value
of learning a critic, is monotone in it: a single $192$-d token loses $34.6$
points and gains $45.1$ from the critic, whereas $196$ patch tokens lose $2.0$
and gain $2.0$.

This also explains why the effect is invisible in published single-horizon
tables, and it makes a concrete prediction for Section~\ref{sec:highdim}: the
critic buys the most exactly where the representation is thinnest. Note the
symmetric reading---on a latent rich enough to make the native distance
informative, even an untuned sampler succeeds ($95.3$--$96.7$ for MPPI on
DINO-WM, against $65.3 \to 47.3$ for the same sampler on LeWM), so the critic
is not what makes planning work in general; it is what makes it work on
representations that do not already encode the geometry.

\paragraph{Refinement is what buys the search budget back.}
Table~\ref{tab:tworoom-budget} holds the cost fixed at the value metric and
varies only how much the world model is queried per decision, measured in
rollout-equivalents ($300$ candidates $\times$ $10$ iterations $= 3{,}000$ for
CEM at the benchmark budget; ${\sim}2$ per RLP refinement step, one forward
unroll and one backward pass). RLP matches CEM to within noise at
${\sim}190\times$ fewer queries, and its budget knob degrades gracefully: $K$
can be halved to $4$ for free on both single-token models, and halved again to
$2$ at a cost of $3.8$ points on PLDM and none on LeWM. CEM's own knob, by
contrast, does nothing between $10$ and $30$ iterations---the extra $6{,}000$
rollouts are wasted.

\paragraph{A reactive policy is not enough.}
The last row of Table~\ref{tab:tworoom-budget} is the empirical counterpart of
Section~\ref{sec:theoretical-results}: a policy trained to emit a plan in a
single pass, by backpropagating the same imagined return through the same
frozen world model against the same critic, and differing from RLP only in that
it does not iterate. It collapses to $31$--$38$ at $h25$ and $15$--$23$ at
$h50$, below every arm in Table~\ref{tab:tworoom-matrix} including the untuned
sampler. Two refinement steps over the identical value landscape recover
$93$--$100$ at a comparable deployment cost. The gap is not explained by how
often the controller re-observes the world---replanning every action block
rather than every five moves it by at most $5.6$ points---so on this task the
refinement recursion~\eqref{eq:plan-rollout}--\eqref{eq:plan-refine}, and not
the learned value alone, is what makes amortized planning work.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Three caveats bound how far these numbers should be read. First, the critic and
the planner are trained on latents cached from the same $10{,}000$ episodes the
evaluation draws its tasks from; different seeds select different tasks, but
every evaluated state lies in the training pool, so all value-metric and RLP
numbers are in-distribution fits rather than generalization estimates
(memorization is implausible---${\sim}150$ evaluated tasks against ${\sim}2$M
valid start--goal pairs and a small MLP head---and the measured effects are an
order of magnitude larger than the optimism attributable to selection at this
sample size, but the held-out card has not been run). The latent-distance rows
involve no learned component beyond the frozen world model and are unaffected.
Second, several arms sit at $99$--$100$, where a $50$-episode cell can no longer
discriminate ($\mathrm{SE} \approx 2.8$ points): differences of two points
inside that band should not be read as ordering. Third, TwoRoom is deliberately
easy for the optimizer---this section is designed to isolate the critic, and
the planner's own contribution is measured in Section~\ref{sec:highdim}.
