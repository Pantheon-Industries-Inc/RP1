**On the same critic, tripling the budget makes CEM worse (−4.22) and Adam better
(+3.11), each 6/6 across seeds and bases.** The dichotomy is not latent-vs-learned
cost — that was the wrong axis, and the latent rows carry no seeds so they never
supported it. It is **sampling vs gradient descent**. A plausible mechanism, and
the one the data fits: CEM refits its population around the elites, so extra
iterations *concentrate* it wherever the quasimetric is most optimistic — a global
hunt for the critic's argmin, i.e. for its error. Adam descends from a fixed init
at a fixed step size; extra restarts or steps buy ordinary convergence inside a
basin and never conduct that hunt. Extra search is only dangerous if the search
is good enough to find the adversarial optimum.

**CEM at 3,000 queries beats Adam at 9,000 by +8.67 (t=4.52, 6/6)** while using
**3× fewer queries and 9× fewer forward-equivalents** (3,000 vs 27,000). At 9,000
each, CEM's edge shrinks to +4.44 and 4/6 — precisely because its own extra
budget degrades it. So Adam's problem is not budget: the two Adam
decompositions are indistinguishable (`[ar]` − `[as]` = +0.67, t=0.47), which
kills both "too few restarts" and "too few steps" as explanations.

**LIPv4 − TD+CEM at the correct budget is +4.11 paired (t=5.57, 6/6)** — smaller
than the +7.56 this file used to claim off the 9,000 baseline, and solid.

Caveat that remains: `lr` stays at the default 0.1 in every Adam arm, so `[as]`'s
90 steps may over-descend at a step size tuned for 30. The `[ar]`≈`[as]` null
makes that less load-bearing than it was.

Note: `budget9k.sh`'s own printed READING block still narrates the superseded
latent-vs-learned story (it was written before these cells existed). This section
is authoritative; the card's prose is not.

### Cost accounting — where "queries/dec" is and isn't the real budget

The counts above are **WM rollouts per decision**, and they are not proportional
to compute, because a gradient solver pays for a backward pass and a sampler
does not. Rollouts are horizon-5. A decision covers
`receding_horizon × action_block` = 5 × 5 = **25 primitive steps**, and
`eval_budget` is 50, so every search planner makes **2 decisions per episode**.
PWM is the exception: exp30 runs it at `receding_horizon=1`, so it replans every
block — **10 decisions per episode**, 5× more often than everything else.

Wall-clock is the median over banked LeWM cells (50 tasks each). "Plan" subtracts
the PWM cell time as an env+render+encode floor.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LIPv4** | 1 × 8 | **17** | 8 | **33** | 8 | **69.4** | **≈2** |
| TD + CEM 3k | 300 × 10 | 3,000 | 0 | 3,000 | 10 | 76.9 | ≈10 |
| latent + CEM 3k | 300 × 10 | 3,000 | 0 | 3,000 | 10 | 87.0 | ≈20 |

The two Adam decompositions cost very differently for identical query counts:
`[as]` runs 1.9× the wall-clock of `[ar]` (295 vs 159 s) because 90 sequential
steps of batch 100 serialize where 30 steps of batch 300 parallelize. They score
the same (79.78 vs 79.33), so `[ar]` strictly dominates `[as]` on
compute-per-point and is the arm to keep if only one is reported. LIPv4 remains
**~1,600× cheaper in forward-equivalents** than the Adam 9k arms and 4.3× cheaper
in wall-clock.

Four things fall out of this that the score table alone hides.

**1. "3,000 queries" already gave Adam 3× CEM's compute.** A backward pass costs
about 2× a forward, so Adam's 3,000 rollouts are ~9,000 forward-equivalents —
the same compute as CEM at 9,000. Measured wall-clock is harsher still: 71 s of
planning against CEM-3k's 10 s, a **7×** gap, because Adam is also 3× deeper
sequentially (30 steps of batch 100 vs 10 steps of batch 300, i.e. more, smaller
kernels). So the finding **"Adam loses to CEM at matched queries" understates the
result** — Adam loses while spending 3–7× the compute. Conversely, the `[ar]` and
`[as]` arms at 9,000 queries are query-matched to CEM-9k but will use ~27,000
forward-equivalents, ~3× its compute; that asymmetry has to be stated whenever
those rows are read.

**2. LIPv4's count was wrong, and it is 17, not ~16.** Per decision the solver
runs 8 refinement iterations, each doing one rollout under `enable_grad` for
∇V plus one `no_grad` rollout for E, then one final rollout to score the selected
candidate: 8 × 2 + 1 = **17 forward rollouts and 8 backward passes** ≈ 33
forward-equivalents. The "~16" came from the `lip.py` docstring's
"~16-20 rollout-equivalents", which counted only the loop's forwards and dropped
both the backward passes and the selection rollout. At `restarts=1`,
`lip_select="last"` and `robust_m=0` — the benchmarked config — there is no
further multiplier; raising any of them scales the final block by `R × C × m`.
Against TD+CEM-3k that is **176× fewer rollouts / 91× less compute**, and against
CEM-9k **529× / 273×**. The corrected number does not dent the claim.

**3. Wall-clock hides almost all of it — the harness is env-bound.** LIPv4 and
CEM-9k differ by 273× in planning compute and by **1.4× in wall-clock** (69 s vs
95 s), because ~67 s of every cell is MuJoCo stepping, EGL rendering and frame
encoding. Any "LIP is faster" claim made on cell time is measuring the simulator,
not the planner. The internal ratios are consistent — CEM 3k→9k triples the
queries and the planning time goes 9.7 s → 27.3 s (2.8×) — so the decomposition
holds up; it is the absolute numbers that are dominated by fixed cost. These
cells also ran 8-way concurrent, so absolutes are inflated and only same-regime
ratios should be trusted.

**4. Unexplained: the latent cost is consistently slower than the learned one**
(87.0 vs 76.9 at 3k, 103.1 vs 94.5 at 9k — same direction at both budgets, so not
noise), even though the TD row runs a critic MLP on top of the *same* input that
the latent row consumes raw. It is **not** a representation mismatch — see the
next section; both costs receive the identical 192-d vector. Cause unknown.

### What the value function actually consumes (verified 2026-08-03)

A single **192-d CLS-token embedding per frame** — not the patch grid, and not
mean-pooled patches.

| stage | shape | |
|---|---|---|
| ViT-tiny encoder output | `(B·T, 257, 192)` | 1 CLS + 256 patch tokens |
| `LeWM.encode` → `[:, 0]` | `(B·T, 192)` | **CLS only; all 256 patch tokens dropped** |
| `projector` MLP 192→2048→192 | `(B, T, 192)` | `info['emb']`, what planners roll out |
| `QuasimetricHead` | 192→256→256→128 | consumes it raw |

The reduction is `pixels_emb = output.last_hidden_state[:, 0]` in
`wm/lewm/lewm.py`, i.e. it happens inside the **world model's encoder**, upstream
of every planner. The metric checkpoints (`lip4_*_value.pt`: `learner=td`,
`latent_dim=192`, `head=quasimetric`, no `compress` key and no `pool_patches`)
load as a bare `QuasimetricHead` — **not** wrapped in `CompressedMetric` — so
there is no pooling, random projection or spatial reduction anywhere in the
metric path. 192-d is simultaneously "the full latent" in the only sense that
matters here: the predictor is 192→192, so dynamics, latent cost, critic and
actor all operate in exactly the same space, and the patch tokens do not exist
downstream of the encoder for any component.

Consequence: **the DINO-WM pooling regression has no analogue on cube/LeWM.**
There is no token grid downstream to mismatch against, so latent-vs-TD, LIP-vs-CEM
and PWM-vs-LIP are all representation-matched by construction. (An earlier draft
of this file speculated the timing anomaly in point 4 was such a mismatch; that
was wrong and is retracted.)

Adam's 9,000 point is being measured **two ways**, because 9,000 queries factors
two different ways for a gradient solver and they are different experiments:
`[ar]` = 300×30 spends the extra 3× on parallel **restarts** (and is CEM's exact
population *and* iteration count, so CEM − `[ar]` is descent-vs-resampling with
nothing else different); `[as]` = 100×90 spends it on **gradient steps** at
Adam's own restart count. Against the banked 100×30 point, `[as]` is the pure
iteration ladder and `[ar]` the pure restart ladder. If TD `[as]` *drops* while
latent `[as]` holds, the over-exploitation turn below is a property of budget
rather than of the sampler — the strongest evidence available that what caps
search-on-a-learned-metric is the metric's error surface. Caveat: `lr` stays at
the default 0.1 in every Adam arm, so a 90-step run may simply over-descend at a
step size tuned for 30 — a confound between "more steps" and "wrong schedule"
that a decaying-lr arm would have to separate.

**More CEM budget makes the learned metric worse and the latent one better.**
On LeWM, TD+CEM goes 85.11 → 82.44 as the iteration count triples (−2.67),
while latent+CEM goes 78.67 → 80.67 (+2.00). The two costs respond to budget in
*opposite directions*, which is the signature of critic exploitation: extra CEM
iterations drive the population into the region where the quasimetric is most
optimistic, and past ~10 iterations that is adversarial search against the
critic's own error rather than better planning. Pure latent distance has no
error to exploit, so it just keeps improving. Two consequences: the correct
CEM operating point for a *learned* cost is not "as much as you can afford", and
**budget-matching cuts LIPv4's margin over TD+CEM from +7.56 to +4.89** — the
honest number is the smaller one.

Two readings that survive the correction. **Adam loses to CEM under both costs at
equal budget** — at 3,000, latent 72.00 vs 78.67 and learned 77.78 vs 85.11; the
9,000 comparison is what `budget9k.sh` is measuring now — so it is a
property of the action landscape, not of the learned metric — a fixed step size
loses to sampling regardless of what it descends. And **LIPv4 has the tightest
spread of any planner** (sd 0.67 / 2.00 against PWM's 16.39), i.e. it is the
most reproducible arm as well as the best — and it reaches 90.00 at 17 rollouts
against TD+CEM's 3,000, a **176× rollout / 91× compute advantage** (see the cost
accounting below) that the budget correction does not touch.

## 2. Why there are two POST arms (the duplication problem)

The WM needs a 25-frame window (span 20 + frameskip 5), so on-policy episodes
under 25 steps are structurally unusable and get dropped. With
`terminate_at_goal=True` and an ~88 %-success actor, **the successes are exactly
the episodes that end early** — so the filter discards successes and keeps
failures:

| | arm 1 "thin" | arm 2 "full" |
|---|---|---|
| `world.terminate_at_goal` | True (default) | **False** |
| episodes kept | 407 of 1800 (22 %) | **1800 of 1800** |
| on-policy rows | 18,500 | **90,000** |
| duplication K for 50/50 | **87** | **18** |
| r1 reference | — | K=25 ✓ same regime |

Arm 1's K=87 means ~10k distinct on-policy windows seen ~174× over two epochs — a
memorisation risk that would make a null uninterpretable. Both arms use
**identical collection seeds**, so `full − thin` isolates the
duplication/termination effect rather than a data reshuffle. The PRE arm is shared.

### What `full − thin` actually changed — row-level, and it is not de-biasing

An earlier draft of this handoff implied `full` fixed a failure-biased dataset.
It did not. Both arms roll out the **same 1800 start states with the same seeds**,
and a failed rollout never terminates early, so it survives the filter in *both*
arms. Splitting thin's 18,500 rows using the measured "72 % of the kept 407 sat
at the 50-step cap" (≈293 timeout episodes; the remaining ≈114 average ≈34 steps,
i.e. successes that landed between step 25 and 50):

| | thin | full |
|---|---|---|
| timeout (failed) episodes | ≈293 | ≈293 — **the same ones** |
| failure rows | **≈14,650** | **≈14,650 — unchanged** |
| success rows | ≈3,850 | ≈75,350 |
| failure share of the 50/50 mix | **≈0.40** | **≈0.08** |

So `full` = thin's failures, untouched, **plus ~75k rows of success trajectory**.
`full − thin` = +2.0 measures *adding successes and cutting duplication 87 → 18*.
Nothing was de-biased, and no failure data was given up.

That matters because it makes the failure share a **suspect, not a fix**. The
[ftmisses campaign](../previous/ftmisses_lipv4_20260715/RESULTS_ftmisses_lipv4.md)
found the WM fine-tune optimum at **90/10 expert:misses** (≈0.10 failure share —
the run that broke the 88-plateau) and that **miss-ONLY destroys the gradient
field** (−20 LIP). The two arms therefore *bracket* a known interior optimum at

### 2.1 The outcome label is recoverable offline — do not re-collect

An earlier draft claimed the existing `onpolicy_full_a*.lance` cannot be divided
by outcome without a 3.3 h re-collect. **That was wrong.** `world.terminateds`
really is useless as a label under `terminate_at_goal=False`, and the goal pose
really was not recorded — but it is *reconstructible*:

- **the drawn tasks are deterministic.** `np.random.default_rng(seed).choice(...)`
  over the post-`ep_range` `valid_indices`. The seeds are known
  (`1000 + a*100 + i`, 36 calls), so which 50 `(episode, start)` pairs each call
  used is pure numpy — no simulation;
- **the recorded↔drawn mapping is unambiguous for arm 2.** `terminate_at_goal=False`
  ⇒ all 50 rollouts reach 50 steps ⇒ none hit `_MIN_LEN=25` ⇒ zero drops ⇒
  recorded episode *k* is drawn task *k*, in order. (Not true for thin: 1393 of
  1800 were dropped and the survivor→task mapping is gone. Thin cannot be
  relabelled, only re-collected — and it does not need to be.);
- **the target is in the expert data** — `privileged_block_0_pos` at
  `(episode, start+25)`, the column `cube.yaml` reads as
  `goal_privileged_block_0_pos`;
- **the recording has `action`.**

Two routes, differing only in one unverified fact — whether `qpos` actually made
it into the recording. Nothing in the tree appears to publish `qpos` into
`world.infos` for the cube env, and the recorder **skips absent columns silently**
(the hazard `world.py`'s own comment warns about), so the lances may be
`pixels`+`action` only despite `qpos,qvel` being in the default column list.
Settle it with `grep 'cols=' /workspace/logs/dsp_colfull_a0_c0.log`:

| route | needs | cost |
|---|---|---|
| arithmetic | `qpos` present → block position directly | seconds |
| replay | only `action` → re-step MuJoCo from the reconstructed init state, **no render, no planner, no WM** | 90k env steps, ~minutes |

**Relabelling is better than re-collecting on the merits, not just cheaper:**
every φ cell is then built from *identical* on-policy rows, so the sweep isolates
the ratio exactly. A fresh collection would reintroduce a data-reshuffle
confound — the very thing the identical-collection-seeds design existed to avoid.

Self-check: recovered labels must come out ≈86–88 % success (the PRE actors' known
rate), ≈293 failed / ≈1507 succeeded. 22 % or 100 % means the `action` rotation
(`_ep['action'].append(_ep['action'].pop(0))`) was undone the wrong way round.

## 3. Validity: the split, and what it did (and didn't) show

There was **no train/eval split anywhere** in this stack — v2WM, the fs1/fs5
caches, the TD teacher, the LIP actor-critic, Dyna collection and the WM
fine-tune all used all 10,000 episodes of `ogb_cube_single.lance`, and
`eval_wm.py` drew its tasks from that same file.

Now enforced for the Dyna control: **train 0–7999 / eval 8000–9999**, strict —
the latent caches are filtered too, so the critic never sees an eval episode.
0–7999 is a *prefix*, so episode ids stay contiguous and `expert_actions.h5` plus
`ep_off/ep_len` bookkeeping keep working with no renumbering. Verified
arithmetically: caches 2,010,000 → 1,608,000 rows = 8000 × 201; eval draw reports
`ep_range 8000:10000 -> 2000/10000 episodes eligible` with 352,000 valid starts =
2000 × 176 exactly.

### The data division, explicitly

Split over the 10,000 episodes of `ogb_cube_single.lance` (201 steps each):
**train = episodes 0–7999 (80 %), eval = episodes 8000–9999 (20 %)**.

| component | episodes used | enforced by |
|---|---|---|
| v2WM base (WM₀) | **ALL 0–9999** ⚠ | not retrained — the one residual leak |
| fs1 latent cache (critic/TD) | 0–7999 → 1,608,000 of 2,010,000 rows | `filter_cache_eprange.py` |
| fs5 latent cache (actor) | 0–7999 → 328,000 rows | `filter_cache_eprange.py` |
| TD teacher | 0–7999 | trained on filtered fs1 |
| LIP actor + critic, **both arms** | 0–7999 | filtered caches |
| Dyna on-policy collection | 0–7999 | `+eval.ep_range=0:8000`, run aborts if not applied |
| WM fine-tune, expert slice | **< 8000** | `build_dyna_mix.py --expert-ep-hi 8000` |
| WM fine-tune, on-policy slice | from 0–7999 rollouts | inherited from collection |
| **evaluation** | **8000–9999 only** | `+eval.ep_range=8000:10000` |

Eval draw: seeds **42/43/44**, `num_eval=50` ⇒ **150 tasks**, goal = frame t+25,
budget 50 steps, success = block within 4 cm. Verified: the draw logs
`ep_range 8000:10000 -> 2000/10000 episodes eligible` and finds **352,000 valid
starts = 2000 × 176** exactly (201 − 25), lowest drawn row 1,642,527 > 1,608,000.

### There is no eval *set* — tasks are manufactured from expert demos

Worth stating plainly, because "held-out episodes" invites the wrong picture.
`ogb_cube_single.lance` (= HF `quentinll/lewm-cube`, the authors' expert
demonstrations, 10k × 201 @ 224px) is a **task generator**, not a set of held-out
trajectories. Per `eval_wm.py:300-388` and `world.py:_extract_init_goal`:

- valid starts are `step_idx ∈ [0, 175]` (`episode_len − goal_offset − 1`), i.e.
  **176 per episode**;
- `np.random.default_rng(cfg.seed).choice(...)` draws 50 `(episode, start)` pairs
  — so **`seed` 42/43/44 *is* the task draw**, not a training seed. That is the
  ~20-pt draw spread in §5.5, and why draw 44 does all the discriminating;
- the 26-frame chunk `[t, t+25]` is split: **init = frame t**, applied by
  `cube.yaml`'s `set_state(qpos, qvel)` callable (the env is teleported into a
  literal MuJoCo snapshot from the expert trajectory); **goal = frame t+25**
  (pixels + `goal_privileged_block_0_pos/quat` → `set_target_pos`).

So the eval distribution is **expert states as starts, 25-step-ahead expert
configurations as goals, at a 2× budget** (50 steps for a 25-step task). Two
consequences: every start state is on the expert manifold, so nothing here tests
recovery from states the expert never visits — which is where the WM is known to
be miss-blind (the graspmiss probe: imagines lift 0.21 on 100 % of real misses vs
reality 0.002). That is a coverage argument for failure data (§0.4) independent of
the mixture question. Minor: `g.choice(len(valid_indices) - 1, ...)` makes the
last valid row undrawable — 1 of 352,000, harmless, but it is a real off-by-one.

Separately, the **fine-tune mixture** (a different "division" — by rows, not
episodes) is **50 / 50 expert : on-policy**, hit by duplicating the on-policy
rows K times:

| arm | expert rows | on-policy rows | K | effective on-policy frac |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| thin | 1,608,000 | 18,500 | 87 | 0.500 |
| **full** | 1,608,000 | **90,000** | **18** | 0.502 |

**The leak turned out to be empirically negligible.** LIP scored **87.8 held-out
vs 86.5 in-pool** — *better* on episodes it never trained on. So critic
memorisation was never the problem. Earlier session notes overstated it; the
value-gradient ablation (87.6 → 54.0, grad carries ~91 %) says only where a leak
*would* bite, not that one was biting.

**Residual, state it in any writeup:** v2WM itself was pre-trained on all 10k
episodes. Fixing that needs a base-WM retrain on a subset — separate job.

**Also unresolved:** `amax` was selected on the eval draws (~3 pts of optimism at
n=150 over ~6 candidates). `dyna_harness/amax_holdout_select.sh` does honest
select-on-42 / report-on-43-44; it picks 1.4 and reports 86.3 vs the naive 86.4,
so the bias was small *because the plateau is flat* — luck, not method.

## 6. Ops gotchas that cost time this session

- **arch-vs-training `config.json`.** `lewm_expert.py` writes the *training*
  config into its checkpoint dir under the name `config.json`; the loader needs
  the *architecture* config. Copying the checkpoint's one kills `cache_latents.py`
  11 s in with `ConfigAttributeError: Missing key load_state_dict`. Fine-tuning
  does not change architecture — **copy `v2WM/config.json`** (byte-identical, sha
  `fd35a47c`, to how the working r1 run packaged its WM). Fixed in both arm
  scripts, with a validator.
- **`eval.ep_range` existed but was broken.** It shrank `ep_indices` without
  shrinking the dataset, so `max_start_per_row` raised `KeyError` on any excluded
  row — unusable for its only purpose. Fixed (`3fbc6f1`) by masking out-of-range
  rows. **The reacher campaign's "held-out draws" are not held out**: its
  collector passes `ep_range=0:8000` against a 1,024-episode file (keeps
  everything — a no-op) and its eval passes no range at all. Its revised
  headlines (LeWM 76.9→78.2, PLDM 78.8→89.2) still carry the leak. Not fixed
  here; that campaign owns those runs.
- **The Bash tool runs zsh**, which does not word-split unquoted `$var`. This
  silently corrupted a test fixture and broke an `ssh $OPTS` invocation. Drive
  test fixtures with `bash script.sh`; inline ssh options rather than putting
  them in a variable.
- **`timeout`-wrapped children get their own pgid**, so killing a driver's
  process group spares them. Exploited deliberately twice to swap a driver
  without losing hours of training — verify with
  `ps -eo pid,pgid,args` before killing.
- **SSH discipline** (from a previous lockout): poll ≥10 min, use
  `ControlMaster`/`ControlPersist`, never retry-loop into a failing connection.
  Concurrent polling loops across sessions saturated sshd `MaxStartups` and
  locked everyone out of every pod.
  **Recurred 2026-07-29 ~03:45–04:30+ UTC**: banner-exchange timeouts on single
  spaced attempts while the composite/PLDM/s12k chain ran unattended — which it
  survived by design (nohup drivers, self-triggering gates, no dependence on
  the operator's connection). Design rule proven: make every multi-hour chain
  operator-connectivity-free.
- **Evals must be sequential.** 3-way → SIGABRT; 2-way silently corrupts (cost an
  8-pt baseline error historically).
- **`pgrep -f "train_lip_a[c]"` matches your own ssh wrapper.** The `[c]` bracket
  protects against pgrep's *own* cmdline, not against the remote parent shell:
  an ssh compound command that mentions `train_lip_ac` anywhere (e.g. a
  verification pgrep at the end) sits in that shell's cmdline for the whole
  session and trips any preflight it launches. Cost one aborted launch
  (2026-07-28 09:00). Launch with a wrapper that never spells out the watched
  patterns; verify in a separate connection.

**New this session, for §0.4** (both tested, neither yet run on the pod):

- `world.py` records a per-step **`success`** column alongside the observation
  columns under `SWM_RECORD_PATH`, so a recorded lance can be divided by outcome.
  It is taken from the env's own flag (`cube_env.post_step` → `_compute_successes`,

  unaffected. The `[record]` line now also prints `kept_success` /
  `drop_success`, which makes the §2 survivorship visible in the log.

  separate rates (`K_f`, `K_s`) so failure share and on-policy share move
  independently; both are shares of **total** mix rows. Omitted or `auto`
  reproduces the historical uniform-`K` build exactly (verified). `--max-dup`
  (default 30) refuses a mix that would repeat a scarce pool into the K=87
  memorisation regime, naming which pool is scarce and how many distinct episodes
  back it. Refusals write nothing and exit 1. Fails with an explicit
  re-collect instruction on a lance that has no `success` column.

  rows, `T = E/(1−f)`, `K_f = φT/F`, `K_s = (f−φ)T/S`. **Measured by the relabel
  pass (2026-07-28): F = 14,450 rows / 289 episodes, S = 75,550 / 1,511** (83.9 %
  success — 4 off the 1,507 prediction; gate B 36/36 clean). An earlier version
  of this paragraph had the K column shifted one φ row — corrected:

  | φ | K_f | verdict |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0 (success-only) | 0 | clean — asks whether failure data matters at all |
  | 0.08 (= full) | 18 | the existing mix |
  | 0.10 (ftmisses optimum) | 22 | clean, but contrast vs 0.08 too small at n=150 |
  | **0.13** | **29** | **max clean φ on today's data** |
  | 0.20 | 45 | trips `--max-dup` — needs +~140 failed eps ≈ **45 min** of collection |
  | 0.40 | 89 | thin's own regime — needs +~570 failed eps ≈ **2.8 h** |

  So the collection needed to unlock clean upper cells is small (~875 / ~3,600
  more episodes at the measured 16 % failure rate). Cheaper still for the WM
  specifically: harvest failures from a deliberately degraded actor (amax 3.5
  fails ~21 %, or add action noise). The WM is a dynamics model — it wants
  state-action coverage, and on-policyness is a *critic* requirement, not a
  dynamics one. Say so in any writeup, because it stops being Dyna in the
  strict sense.
