# Reacher at the published replanning cadence (rh=1) — 2026-08-04

Status: screens complete, final actors training, report card pending.
Everything below is on **selection seeds 50–51** and is therefore a
*selection* result, not a reported one. The reporting-seed card (42–47) is
stage 3 and is not in this document yet.

---

## 0. Why this exists

The reported table (`RESULTS_PAPER_20260802.md`) runs `receding_horizon=5`: the
whole 25-step plan executes before replanning, two planning cycles per episode.
DINO-WM, PLDM and stable-worldmodel all replan after a short chunk. An rh=1 row
is therefore needed, and it plausibly *disfavours* RLP — frequent replanning is
exactly what lets a cheap sampler correct its own errors.

The ablation had been blocked since 2026-08-02 on an evaluation readout (I3)
that never executed.

---

## 1. The blocker: I3 was in a module the RLP arm does not run

The handoff prescribed one diagnostic — print unconditionally at the entry of
`_MetricCost.get_cost` in `eval_wm.py`. That was already answerable from the
existing artifact: **`logs/i3dbg.log` contains no `[metric-hook]` line**, and
that print sits unconditionally at the method's entry. `_MetricCost.get_cost`
is never called in a LIP cell.

`LIPSolver` never calls `model.get_cost` on its refinement path. It scores with
`self.lip_value` — the value embedded in the actor checkpoint — through
`LIPSolver._V`. Four successive edits had gone into `_MetricCost`, which the RLP
arm does not execute.

This also resolves the handoff's flagged implication. Both cost paths are now
located:

| arm family | cost path | window stacking |
|---|---|---|
| Latent/Value + CEM/Adam | `_MetricCost.get_cost`, `_m >= 3` branch | explicit frame concat |
| RLP (`solver=lip`) | `LIPSolver._V` | `window_pair` |

The reported 3-frame numbers were computed through these two paths, not through
an unlocated one.

### Two further defects, both hidden by the dead code

- **Frame axis.** `predicted_emb` carries `T = off + P` frames, not `P`: the WM
  re-predicts its conditioning history, so a 5-block plan produces a 6-long
  axis and the plan chunks are the *last* 5. The guard compared
  `chunks_remaining` against `T`. At rh=5 the count reaches 5 at the second
  replan and `5 < 6` is true — I3 would have fired **inside the reported
  protocol**. It now compares against `P`, with indices offset by `T - P`.
  `LIPSolver._V` needs no offset; its trajectory is the plan exactly.
- **Goal broadcast.** The prefix stack prepends an axis to `pred`; the generic
  `goal.unsqueeze(1)` below produced `(J,1,B,D)` against `(J,B,S,D)` and every
  Latent+CEM cell crashed outright.

### Readout semantics, corrected on evidence

The handoff specified `cost = min_j V(z_j, z_g)` over the reachable prefix. That
is not the objective held-at-end grades — it rewards passing near the goal at
any reachable chunk, while the metric asks where the arm *is* on the graded
step. Measured on the handoff's own reference cell: `min` gave 24.0 @0.05
against an unaligned control of 42.0. Scoring the chunk that lands on the
deadline gives 96.0 @0.1 against the control's 86.0. Both live behind
`I3_MODE` (`deadline` default, `min` retained).

### What this says

**The two fixes are substitutes, and combining them is harmful.** I3 rescues an
unaligned actor (pldm terminal_a22: 37 → 99 @0.05) and damages an aligned one
(pldm dense_a22: 94 → 30). The reading: a terminal-trained actor leaves its plan
prefix uncontrolled, so at rh=1 the readout has to compensate; an aligned actor
already controls every step, and I3 then hands its learned refinement a cost it
never saw in training.

**`randhorizon_a22` wins on both bases**, and is the only mode to win at the
same `amax` on both. Its rationale is the one that matches the deployment:
`V(z_h, z_g)` with `h ~ U{1..5}` asks every prefix to land, and at rh=1 any
chunk can be the one that executes. Note its training loss is the *highest* of
the four modes (`E_final` 1.38–1.86 vs dense's 1.01–1.17) — it scores a random
prefix, a different quantity, and loss and score decouple on this task.

## 6. The samplers were not collapsing

The handoff carried a partial, explicitly non-quotable finding: at rh=1,
"RLP 92.8 (n=36) but Latent+CEM 58.3 (n=6) — samplers appear to collapse under
frequent replanning while RLP is near-invariant. If real this *widens* RLP's
margin."

It is not real. Sampling arms at rh=1, selection seeds:

| arm | I3 off | I3 deadline | I3 min |
|---|---|---|---|
| Latent+CEM lejepa | 32.0 | **87.0** | 82.0 |
| Latent+CEM pldm | 28.0 | **86.0** | 80.0 |
| Value+CEM lejepa | 15.0 | **48.0** | 43.0 |
| Value+CEM pldm | 16.0 | **40.0** | 40.0 |

Given the deadline-aware readout, the bar recovers to 87.0 @0.1 — close to its
reported rh=5 value of 84.3. The apparent collapse was the same misalignment
that afflicted RLP, measured on an arm nobody had given the fix to.

**This is why the readout had to be screened on the baselines separately.**
I3 is worth ~55 points to Latent+CEM and nothing to an aligned RLP; adopting it
globally on RLP's evidence would have inflated the margin by that much.

**Protocol adopted for the card:** every arm is carded under the readout that is
best *for that arm*, chosen on the selection seeds — RLP with I3 off, every
sampling arm with I3=deadline. This is the choice conservative for the claim.

## 7. Step count

The screen's actors used 6000 steps; the reported recipe uses 1000. The two are
confounded, so both were measured.

- Under a **terminal** loss, 6000 steps *hurts* at rh=1 (lejepa 79.0 → 51.0
  @0.1), confirming the retrain script's own warning that a longer budget
  over-optimises a terminal-only cost.
- Under an **aligned** loss it helps: dense@1000 vs dense@6000 is 98.0/80.0 vs
  100.0/95.0 (lejepa) and 98.0/90.0 vs 100.0/94.0 (pldm).

Coherent — a path-constraining loss does not over-optimise the terminal. The
final recipe uses 6000 steps, and the step count is therefore a second
deliberate difference from the reported recipe, not an accident.
