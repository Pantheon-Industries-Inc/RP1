\subsection{Planning in Higher Dimensions}
\label{sec:highdim}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Reacher is the two-link arm of the DeepMind Control Suite
\citep{tassa2018deepmind}, observed as $224\times224$ RGB, with a $2$-d torque
action; as in Section~\ref{sec:tworoom} a plan is $N=5$ action blocks of
$F=5$ primitive steps and the whole sequence executes before replanning. Two
things make it a harder test of the optimizer than TwoRoom. The action space is
continuous torque rather than velocity, so the map from plan to terminal state is
not close to an integrator; and we score with a \emph{held-at-end} criterion---
every joint within $\tau$ of the goal \emph{at the final step}, rather than at
any point during the episode. Held-at-end is strictly harder than the latched
convention the released code reports, because it penalizes trajectories that
sweep through the goal and drift off, and it is the criterion under which
settling behaviour is visible at all: in the offline corpus only $0.4\%$ of
at-goal states are still at goal $25$ steps later. We report
$\tau \in \{0.1, 0.05\}$ rad. Tasks replay offline trajectories exactly as in
Section~\ref{sec:tworoom} with $\Delta = 25$; evaluation draws from a
held-apart pool of $2{,}000$ episodes, $50$ tasks per cell, and every learned
row is $6$ training seeds $\times$ $6$ task seeds ($36$ evaluations). We use the
two single-token world models, whose latent is $192$-d.

\paragraph{Here the critic is not what helps---and that is the point.}
Reacher inverts the ordering of Section~\ref{sec:tworoom}. Swapping the latent
distance for the value metric under a \emph{sampling} planner does not help; it
hurts, and substantially, on both models ($84.3 \to 51.3$ on LeWM,
$78.3 \to 51.3$ on PLDM at $\tau = 0.1$). The same substitution was worth
$+45.1$ and $+22.3$ points on TwoRoom. Nothing about the critic is degenerate
here---it is the same architecture, the same TD objective, and it is the object
RLP optimizes against while attaining the best numbers in the table. What
differs is that Reacher's latent geometry is already adequate for the horizon
being planned over: the arm has no barriers and no irreversible transitions, so
squared latent distance is a serviceable local surrogate for steps-to-go at
$\Delta = 25$, and a learned quasimetric fit to $50$-step returns is a noisier
readout of that same ordering. The two environments therefore separate the two
contributions of this paper rather than confirming one twice. TwoRoom is where
the objective is wrong and the critic fixes it; Reacher is where the objective
is adequate and the critic is, on its own, a liability. That RLP nevertheless
leads by $+13.9$ and $+15.9$ points over the best hand-designed planner---while
optimizing the \emph{worse} of the two cost surfaces---is attributable to the
search, and to nothing else in the pipeline.

\paragraph{Refinement covers a search space that sampling cannot.}
At $\tau = 0.1$ RLP reaches $98.2$ and $94.2$ against the strongest baseline's
$84.3$ and $78.3$, at ${\sim}16$ queries against $3{,}000$---a
${\sim}190\times$ reduction, matching the ratio observed in
Section~\ref{sec:tworoom} on a task where the baseline is not saturated and the
margin is therefore visible. The gap is not an artifact of under-resourcing the
sampler. Tripling CEM's budget to $9{,}000$ queries ($300 \times 30$, the
released iteration count) moves the latent-distance rows only to $87.3$ and
$84.0$, leaving RLP ahead by $+10.9$ and $+10.2$ at $562\times$ fewer queries;
the marginal value of a sampled candidate is near zero long before the gap
closes, because the difficulty is not density of coverage but where in the
$10$-d block space the coverage is placed. The tolerance columns say the same
thing more sharply. Tightening $\tau$ from $0.1$ to $0.05$ costs CEM $39.6$
points and RLP $31.9$, so the ordering is preserved but the \emph{ratio} widens:
at $\tau = 0.05$ RLP is $1.48\times$ the best sampler on LeWM and $1.45\times$
on PLDM, against $1.17\times$ and $1.20\times$ at $\tau = 0.1$. Precision at the
terminal state is exactly what a zeroth-order proposal distribution is worst at,
and exactly what a learned refinement step can be trained to supply.

\paragraph{The recursion, again, is the active ingredient.}
The last row repeats the ablation of Section~\ref{sec:tworoom} under the
strictest possible matching: the reactive policy is trained for the same
$1{,}000$ steps as RLP, through the same frozen model, against the same critic
and the same \emph{terminal} objective, differing only in that it emits its plan
in a single pass. It reaches $64.1$ and $20.4$---below the latent-distance
sampler on both models, and $34$ to $74$ points below RLP. Two observations from
the same sweep sharpen the reading. Given $8\times$ the optimization budget the
terminal-objective reactive policy does not improve but \emph{collapses}
($64.1 \to 0.3$ on LeWM), the signature of a policy driving into world-model
error when its only feedback is one scalar at the most extrapolated latent;
whereas a dense per-step variant improves with the same budget
($81.2 \to 85.9$). RLP is terminal in the same sense and also degrades with
longer training---$1{,}000$ steps beat $3{,}000$ in all twelve
(model, learning rate, path-weight) pairings we swept---so its step count is
best read as early stopping against this failure mode rather than as a tuned
constant. The refinement recursion is what makes a terminal signal usable at
all: eight steps of it turn the same objective that destroys a one-pass policy
into the best planner in the table.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
As in Section~\ref{sec:tworoom} the critic and planner are fit on latents cached
from episodes the evaluation draws from, so the headline rows are
in-distribution fits. Here we measure the resulting optimism rather than argue
it away: retraining both components on a disjoint $80\%$ of episodes and
re-scoring on the held-apart remainder costs $0.5$ points on LeWM and $2.7$ on
PLDM ($97.7 \pm 1.6$ and $91.5 \pm 6.0$), an order of magnitude below the
margins being claimed. The world models themselves are pretrained on the full
corpus and are shared by every row including the parameter-free latent-distance
baselines, which have no capacity to memorize; the comparison is therefore
unaffected even though the absolute numbers are upper bounds. Two further
caveats: the $\tau = 0.05$ column carries large across-seed spread
($\pm 8.7$ and $\pm 12.5$) and single-seed differences inside it should not be
ordered; and we do not report the patch-token model on this task, for the
mechanical reason given in Appendix~\ref{app:reacher}.

\paragraph{The discount and the window.}
Two settings distinguish this critic from TwoRoom's and both are load-bearing.
The discount is sharply peaked at $\gamma = 0.98$: both neighbours lose $4$--$5$
points and $\gamma = 0.95$ falls below the latent-distance baseline. Discounting
also improves the critic \emph{as a cost in its own right}---Value$+$CEM rises
from $38.3$ to $51.3$ (LeWM) and $33.7$ to $51.3$ (PLDM)---so it is correcting
the value, not merely conditioning the planner's optimization. The temporal
window is worth $8$--$22$ points and helps the parameter-free baseline too
($84.3$ vs $73.0$ on LeWM with a single frame), which locates it in the cost
function rather than in what the refiner can exploit: held-at-end asks whether
the arm is \emph{still} at the goal, and a single latent frame carries no
velocity information, whereas three frames spaced one action block apart do. The
window is not free---at training time the goal window is three genuine
consecutive frames, at deployment only one goal frame exists and is tiled, a
mismatch we measure at $7.02$ against a random-pair baseline of $82.19$---but it
is clearly favourable.

\paragraph{Deviations and caveats.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt \parskip0pt \topsep2pt
  \item \textbf{Patch-token model not run.} DINO-WM's latent on this task is
    $196$ tokens $\times$ $384 = 75{,}264$-d, unpooled, so a full-corpus
    latent cache is $605$ GB. The critic and planner both require the cache;
    the documented remedy is a fixed random projection to $1{,}024$-d, which
    preserves the L2 geometry the latent-distance baseline depends on where
    mean-pooling would not. Not run. The parameter-free baseline needs no cache
    but its $3$-frame metric hook is incompatible with this model's rollout
    interface, which supplies two frames.
  \item \textbf{Critic not held fixed}, as in Appendix~\ref{app:tworoom}: it is
    warm-started from the TD solution and co-trained. Its data remains cached
    transitions only---value expansion is off ($0$)---so no learned component
    trains on states the planner itself visits.
  \item \textbf{Reactive-policy objective.} The reported row uses the terminal
    objective to match RLP. Its released default is a dense per-step objective,
    which scores $81.2$ / $91.2$ at the same budget and $85.9$ / $99.0$ at the
    released $8{,}000$ steps. Those figures are an ablation, not a baseline:
    dense per-timestep supervision is an objective RLP does not have, and
    comparing across it would confound architecture with objective.
  \item \textbf{World-model fine-tuning does not help here.} A Dyna round
    \eqref{eq:dyna-fixpoint} on a $50/50$ mix of offline and on-policy data
    improves the latent-distance baseline ($84.3 \to 86.3$) and \emph{degrades}
    RLP ($89.0 \to 75.1$, $5$ seeds), inverting the margin. The fine-tune
    sharpens the geometry L2 exploits while degrading the learned value
    (Value$+$CEM $17.7 \to 14.0$). Excluded from the main table; it is also the
    only experiment in this paper that touches the environment.
  \item \textbf{Optimiser settings are script-provenance.} Neither trainer
    serialises learning rate, step count, expectile, discount or replay
    probability. Architectures are checkpoint-verified; the fields marked
    $^\dagger$ above are reproducible from the release but not independently
    attestable from an artifact. Two critics differing only in expectile are
    indistinguishable from their checkpoints.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Reproduction.}
Two failure modes cost measurable accuracy. Rendering through \texttt{osmesa}
rather than EGL costs ${\approx}7$ points on this task, so the renderer is
pinned and evaluations are serialised per GPU rather than switched. And
concurrent sampling-solver evaluations core-dump under GPU contention; because a
crashed cell yields no score and a naive aggregation averages it as a zero, a
crash is indistinguishable from a genuine total failure. Every cell we report
records how many task seeds actually scored, and all reported cells are
complete. Reference environment: torch \texttt{2.6.0+cu124}, transformers
\texttt{5.14.1}, mujoco \texttt{3.10.0}, dm\_control \texttt{1.0.43},
Python \texttt{3.11}.
