\paragraph{Deep Model-Predictive Optimization (DMPO).}
DMPO keeps the MPPI update and learns a residual on it: a network reads the sampling
distribution and the $N$ rollout costs (no state, no gradient) and emits a gated mean
correction, a covariance update, and a learned warm-start shift \citep{sacks2024deep}.
We train it with the paper's objective, PPO, closed inside the frozen world model
against the same critic $V_\psi$ the cell's RLP row deploys, for the paper's budget of
$1000$ policy-improvement iterations. Both baselines are given the protocol we give our
own method: three training seeds, snapshots taken through training (here every $200$
iterations), and \emph{one} shared snapshot per cell chosen by the mean score over seeds
on the validation draws, ties broken toward the earlier snapshot -- the same rule, on the
same draws, by which RLP selects its (teacher, actor) pair. Reporting a fixed final
iterate would not be equal treatment: DMPO's PPO return is a trendless walk over its
budget, and its final iterate is selected in only one of ten cells. We train DMPO with a
budget of $256$ rollouts per decision ($256{\times}1$).

\paragraph{Learning-to-Optimize MPC (L2O-MPC).}
L2O-MPC, DMPO's predecessor, learns the \emph{whole} sampling update rather than a
residual: a network reads the mean, covariance, and $N$ costs and emits a gated
replacement mean \citep{sacks2022learning}. As it is not a working optimizer untrained,
it is trained by DAgger imitation of a larger-budget MPPI expert, computed here through
the same frozen world model and critic. We match the paper's DAgger structure exactly --
$20$ rounds, $\beta_k = 0.8^k$, and an expert budget far above the learner's ($N{=}512$
against $M{=}64$) -- but not its literal optimizer budget of roughly $10^6$ gradient
steps per seed: in the original setting a step is a small MLP update, whereas here it is
a world-model unroll. We instead train to convergence, $18{,}000$ steps, snapshot every
$3{,}000$, and select as above; the per-cell selection curves in
Table~\ref{tab:baselines} show the objective flat over the final snapshots in all but two
cells, whose reported scores are therefore lower bounds. We train L2O-MPC with a budget
of $256$ rollouts per decision ($64{\times}4$), matching DMPO.
